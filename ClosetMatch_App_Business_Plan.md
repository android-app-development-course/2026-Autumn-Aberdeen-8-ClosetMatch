# ClosetMatch App Business Plan## 1 Executive SummaryClosetMatch is a smart wardrobe management and outfit recommendation app designed for university students and young adults. Its slogan is “Your clothes, more than one way to match.”Users can take or upload photos of their clothes. The app automatically identifies information such as clothing category, color, style, and suitable season. After choosing the weather and occasion, users receive several outfit recommendations based on the clothes they already own.The main purpose of ClosetMatch is to help users make better use of their existing clothes, reduce repeated outfits, and spend less time deciding what to wear. The app includes a user account system, but payment and social community features are not included in the current development plan.## 2 App Overview### 2.1 App NameChinese name: 衣橱搭搭English name: ClosetMatchSlogan: Your clothes, more than one way to match.### 2.2 App PositioningClosetMatch is an intelligent outfit assistant that generates recommendations from the user’s real wardrobe.After creating and signing in to an account, users can take a photo of an item or select an image from their phone. Image recognition is used to generate labels for the clothing category, color, style, and season. Users can check and correct these labels before saving the item to their virtual wardrobe.When users want an outfit recommendation, they select the current weather and occasion. The app then considers clothing categories, colors, styles, usage frequency, and recent outfit history to generate several suitable combinations.### 2.3 Problems to Be SolvedThe target users commonly experience the following problems:- They spend too much time deciding what to wear.- They own many clothes but repeatedly wear the same combinations.- Some clothes remain unused for long periods.- Online fashion recommendations often include clothes the user does not own.- Manually entering the category, color, and style of every item takes too much time.- A single recommendation may not match the user’s personal preference.- Clothes that are no longer owned or liked need to be removed from the virtual wardrobe.### 2.4 Core Features#### User Account SystemUsers can create an account, sign in, and sign out. Wardrobe information, preferences, and outfit history are connected to the user’s account.#### Automatic Clothing RecognitionWhen a user takes or uploads a clothing photo, the app automatically generates labels for:- Clothing category, such as top, trousers, skirt, coat, or shoes- Main and secondary colors- Clothing style, such as casual, sporty, formal, minimalist, or sweet- Suitable season or temperatureUsers can manually edit the labels if the recognition result is incorrect.#### Virtual WardrobeThe virtual wardrobe displays all saved items by category. Users can view clothing photos and labels and edit the information when necessary.#### Clothing DeletionUsers can delete an item when they no longer own or like it. A confirmation message is displayed before deletion to prevent accidental operations. Deleted items will no longer appear in future outfit recommendations.#### Weather and Occasion SelectionBefore generating an outfit, users can select the current weather and occasion.Weather options may include:- Hot- Warm- Cool- Cold- Sunny- RainyOccasion options may include:- Class- Daily commute- Party- Date- Exercise- Interview#### Multiple Outfit RecommendationsAfter the user selects the weather and occasion, the app generates three to five different outfit combinations at the same time.Each recommendation displays the selected clothing items, a short explanation, and the suitable occasion. Users can compare the recommendations and choose their preferred outfit. They can also replace one item or regenerate all recommendations.#### Outfit HistoryThe app records outfits that users have selected or worn. Recent combinations receive a lower recommendation priority, helping users avoid repeating the same outfits too frequently.#### User FeedbackUsers can respond to a recommendation by selecting “Like,” “Dislike,” “Worn,” or “Generate Again.” This feedback can be used to improve later recommendations.### 2.5 Minimum Viable ProductThe Minimum Viable Product, or MVP, will include:- Account registration and login- Clothing photo capture and upload- Automatic category, color, and style recognition- Manual correction of recognition results- Virtual wardrobe management- Clothing information editing and deletion- Weather and occasion selection- Three to five outfit recommendations at one time- Single-item replacement- Outfit history- Basic user feedbackPayment, subscriptions, social communities, chat, public posts, and friend-following features will not be included in the first version.## 3 Identify a Unique Selling PropositionThe Unique Selling Proposition of ClosetMatch is:**Automatically identify the clothes users already own and provide several practical, non-repetitive outfit choices based on the weather and occasion.**### 3.1 Automatic Label GenerationUsers do not need to enter every clothing detail manually. The app uses image recognition to generate category, color, style, and season labels, making wardrobe creation faster and easier.### 3.2 Recommendations Based on Real ClothesEvery recommendation uses clothing that the user has added to the virtual wardrobe. The app does not suggest items that the user does not own.### 3.3 Several Choices at One TimeInstead of providing only one result, ClosetMatch generates three to five different outfits. Users can compare the options and select the one that best matches their needs.### 3.4 Fewer Repeated OutfitsThe app checks recent outfit history and reduces the priority of combinations that were recently worn.### 3.5 Better Wardrobe UtilizationClosetMatch records how frequently each item is recommended and selected. When appropriate, less frequently used items receive a higher recommendation priority.### 3.6 Weather and Occasion AwarenessThe recommendation process considers the weather and occasion in addition to color and style. This helps the app remove unsuitable clothing before generating the final results.## 4 Target a User Persona### 4.1 Target UsersThe main target users have the following characteristics:- Age: 18 to 28- Identity: university students, young employees, and young adults living independently- Device: Android smartphone- Situation: they own enough daily clothes but lack outfit ideas or do not want to spend much time choosing clothes- Need: they want to enter clothes quickly and choose from several practical recommendations### 4.2 User PersonaLin is a 20-year-old university student. She owns many everyday clothes but often wears the same familiar combinations to class. She wants to try different styles but does not want to continue buying new clothes.After creating a ClosetMatch account, Lin takes photos of her clothes. The app automatically identifies their categories, colors, and styles, so she only needs to check whether the labels are correct.Before going to class, she selects the current weather and chooses “Class” as the occasion. ClosetMatch generates several suitable outfits. Lin can compare them, choose her favorite combination, or replace an item she does not want to wear.If she no longer likes or owns an item, she can delete it from her virtual wardrobe.## 5 Market Need and Competitive PositioningUsers currently obtain outfit ideas from short-video platforms, fashion communities, online shopping platforms, and fashion influencers. However, these channels have several limitations:- The recommended clothes may not be owned by the user.- The content often focuses on fashion trends or product sales instead of wardrobe utilization.- Users must convert general fashion inspiration into a practical outfit themselves.- Most platforms do not record personal outfit history or clothing usage.- Some wardrobe apps require users to enter too much information manually.- A single recommendation does not provide enough choice.ClosetMatch is positioned as a personal wardrobe management and outfit decision-making tool. Its purpose is not to encourage users to buy more clothes. Instead, it helps users discover more ways to wear what they already own.## 6 Product Operation ModelClosetMatch will initially operate as a free app. The early goal is to test whether the core functions solve real user problems.The current version will not include payments, subscriptions, in-app purchases, social communities, public posts, or chat functions. Development will focus on the user account system, automatic clothing recognition, wardrobe management, clothing deletion, and multiple outfit recommendations.If testing shows that users continue to use the app, later versions can improve recognition accuracy, recommendation quality, and data synchronization.## 7 Use the Right Marketing Channels### 7.1 XiaohongshuContent such as “Different Ways to Wear the Same Item,” “A Week Without Repeating an Outfit,” and “How to Make Better Use of Your Wardrobe” can demonstrate the value of ClosetMatch.The content should show the real process of uploading clothes, generating labels, and receiving several outfit recommendations.### 7.2 Douyin and Other Short-Video PlatformsShort videos can demonstrate the complete process:1. Take a clothing photo.2. Automatically generate clothing labels.3. Select the weather and occasion.4. Receive several outfit recommendations.5. Choose or edit one recommendation.The videos should emphasize convenience, automatic recognition, and multiple choices.### 7.3 BilibiliLonger videos can present the app development process, recognition tests, recommendation rules, and complete product demonstrations. This channel can reach users interested in fashion, productivity tools, and mobile app development.### 7.4 Campus PromotionThe development team can recruit testers through classmates, dormitory groups, student organizations, and campus communities. Students can install the app, complete several tasks, and provide direct feedback.### 7.5 App Store OptimizationThe app description should highlight keywords such as:- Automatic clothing recognition- Multiple outfit recommendations- Weather and occasion matching- Virtual wardrobe- Outfit historyApp store screenshots should show the automatic labels and recommendation results instead of only displaying decorative interface elements.## 8 Product Development Plan### Stage 1 Account System and Basic NavigationDevelop account registration, login, logout, the home screen, and basic page navigation.Acceptance requirement: users can create an account, sign in, and open the wardrobe and recommendation pages.### Stage 2 Intelligent Clothing EntryAllow users to take or upload photos. Add automatic recognition for clothing category, color, and style, with an option to correct the generated labels.Acceptance requirement: users can save an item with automatically generated and manually corrected information.### Stage 3 Virtual Wardrobe ManagementDevelop wardrobe display, category filtering, clothing editing, and deletion.Acceptance requirement: saved clothes remain available after the app is closed and reopened, and deleted clothes no longer appear in the wardrobe.### Stage 4 Multiple Outfit RecommendationsAdd weather and occasion selection. Generate three to five different outfit combinations and display the reason for each recommendation.Acceptance requirement: the app can generate several clearly different and usable outfits from the user’s wardrobe.### Stage 5 Replacement and Outfit HistoryAllow users to replace one clothing item, regenerate recommendations, save selected outfits, and view outfit history.Acceptance requirement: recent outfits are recorded and considered when new recommendations are generated.### Stage 6 Testing and ImprovementTest account management, automatic recognition, clothing deletion, outfit generation, and feedback functions. Fix major problems and prepare a complete product demonstration.## 9 Channels to Validate an AppAfter completing the basic version, the team will invite at least ten classmates to install and use ClosetMatch.Each tester will be asked to:1. Create and sign in to an account.2. Add at least five clothing items.3. Check the automatically generated category, color, and style labels.4. Correct at least one inaccurate label.5. Select the weather and an occasion.6. Review several outfit recommendations.7. Choose one preferred outfit.8. Delete one test clothing item.9. Submit comments and suggestions.The team will record the following information:| Test Area | Information to Record ||---|---|| Account system | Whether registration and login work correctly || Automatic recognition | Whether category, color, and style labels are accurate || Clothing entry | Whether the process is simple and how long it takes || Multiple recommendations | Whether the number and variety of outfits are appropriate || Recommendation quality | Whether the outfits are reasonable and wearable || Clothing deletion | Whether the function is easy to find and understand || Technical problems | Errors, crashes, slow responses, or confusing operations || Suggestions | Features or changes requested by users |After testing, the team will summarize the feedback from at least ten classmates and identify problems mentioned by multiple users.Issues affecting registration, clothing entry, label recognition, deletion, and outfit generation will receive the highest priority. Minor visual improvements can be completed later.## 10 Main Risks and Solutions### 10.1 Inaccurate Recognition ResultsUsers will be allowed to edit automatically generated labels. During testing, the team will record which clothing types are frequently identified incorrectly and improve the recognition process.### 10.2 Slow Image RecognitionThe app will display a clear loading message during recognition. If recognition fails or times out, users can enter the labels manually.### 10.3 Unreasonable Outfit RecommendationsRule-based filtering will remove combinations that do not match the weather, occasion, or required clothing categories before the final results are generated.### 10.4 Recommendations Are Too SimilarThe recommendation system will apply a difference requirement so that each outfit contains meaningful changes. Different options may focus on casual, minimalist, sporty, or formal styles.### 10.5 User Data LossWardrobe information and outfit history will be connected to the correct user account. The app must also clearly explain how clothing photos are stored and used.### 10.6 Accidental Clothing DeletionA confirmation message will appear before deletion. If development time allows, an undo option can also be added.### 10.7 Development Takes Longer Than ExpectedThe team will prioritize the account system, automatic clothing recognition, wardrobe management, clothing deletion, and multiple outfit recommendations. Payment and social features will remain outside the project scope.## 11 Project GoalsThe project will answer the following questions:1. Are users willing to create a virtual wardrobe by taking photos of their clothes?2. Does automatic label generation reduce the time required to add clothes?3. Are the generated category, color, and style labels accurate enough?4. Do users prefer receiving several outfit recommendations instead of only one?5. Do weather and occasion information improve the usefulness of recommendations?6. What common problems and suggestions are reported by at least ten student testers?The short-term goal of ClosetMatch is to deliver a working app that allows users to create an account, add clothes with automatic labels, manage and delete wardrobe items, and receive several outfit recommendations.The feedback collected from student testers will then be used to improve recognition accuracy, interface usability, and recommendation quality.
